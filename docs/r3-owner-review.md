## What is being asked of the reviewer

Every question below is `contested` and every plan is `draft`, and both
stay that way until this review happens. Three separate decisions:

1. **Is the wording right?** The `clarified_ask` is what the plan was
   written against. If it does not say what the `ask` means, the plan is
   solving a different problem and its result is not evidence.
2. **Are the labelled cards right?** Required means every one is needed;
   any-of means one suffices; forbidden means returning it is wrong;
   counterexample means retrieving it is fine and recommending it is not.
3. **Is the plan expressing the ask, or fitting the answer?** A bound
   chosen after seeing where the answer ranked is tuning, not retrieval.

(1) IT IS A DELAYED TRIGGERED ABILITY, CREATED WHEN PACT RESOLVES. Pact's second sentence is not part of the counter effect; as Pact resolves it creates an ability scheduled to act later. Any one of 603.7, 603.7a or 610.2 establishes this, which is why they are a disjunction rather than a conjunction. 603.7a adds the sharpest detail — delayed triggered abilities are created during the resolution of spells or abilities — which fixes the moment of creation at Pact's resolution.

(2) THE OBLIGATION SURVIVES PACT LEAVING THE STACK, AND IT BINDS THE CASTER. This is the proposition 603.7d and only 603.7d carries: the source of the delayed triggered ability is the spell, and its controller is the player who controlled that spell as it resolved. (3) TIMING, AND ONLY ONCE. 603.7b settles that the ability triggers only once — the next time its trigger event occurs — because it has no stated duration. P1 — TARGET RESTRICTION: WHY A SPELL CAN BE TARGETED AT ALL. Spells are not targetable by default. Only permanents are legal targets for spells and abilities unless the effect specifies another zone or a player, or targets an object that can't exist on the battlefield, such as a spell (115.2). Commandeer's "target noncreature spell" is exactly that explicit exception. Carrier: 115.2, which is the only rule in the document that states the only-permanents default and its exception.

P2 — TARGET RESTRICTION: WHAT THE TARGET MUST BE. Because the target description includes the word "spell," it means a spell matching that description ON THE STACK (109.2b). Two consequences a set must support: the target must be an object that is currently a spell, so Commandeer cannot hit a permanent, a card in a graveyard, or a spell that has already resolved or been countered; and "noncreature" is a CARD-TYPE filter on that spell, so artifact, enchantment, planeswalker, battle, instant and sorcery spells are legal and a creature spell is not. Carrier: 109.2b. Corroborating but NOT required: 601.2c (targets chosen as the spell is cast) and 608.2b (legality rechecked on resolution, and if the target is gone Commandeer does not resolve). OPTIONAL ELABORATION, not part of P2: that an artifact creature spell is still a creature spell and therefore illegal follows from 205.2b ("Some objects have more than one card type (for example, an artifact creature). Such objects satisfy the criteria for any effect that applies to any of their card types.") or 300.2. A set is NOT marked insufficient for lacking it.

P3 — WHAT CONTROL CHANGE DOES: THE EFFECT NOW HAPPENS FOR YOU. You are now the spell's controller, so you are the player who follows its instructions as it resolves (608.2c), and/or every "you"/"your" in its text now refers to you (109.5). EITHER carries P3. (608.2c is the broader of the two: 109.5 only reaches text that says "you"/"your," so it does nothing for a commandeered "Destroy target creature.") A set with neither leaves "you get the effect" unsupported.

P4 — WHAT CONTROL CHANGE DOES: WHO GETS THE PERMANENT. If the commandeered spell is a permanent spell, you get the permanent. EITHER citation carries P4: 110.2b, the on-point rule for a player gaining control of another player's permanent spell; or 608.3a, under which a resolving permanent spell with no targets becomes a permanent and enters the battlefield under the control of the spell's controller — which, once P3 has made you the controller, settles it for the ordinary untargeted case (an Aura spell routes through 608.3c, "is put onto the battlefield under the control of the spell's controller"). 110.2b additionally states the half that is easy to miss — the permanent's controller BY DEFAULT is still the player who put the spell on the stack, which matters only in the multiplayer leaves-the-game case (800.4c). That extra content is a bonus, not a requirement.

P5 — WHAT CONTROL CHANGE DOES NOT CHANGE: OWNERSHIP. Control is not ownership, and both halves must be supported. (a) The concepts are distinct and ownership tracks the card: a spell's owner is the owner of the card that represents it, while a spell's controller is only BY DEFAULT the player who put it on the stack (112.2) — the "by default" is also what makes a control-changing effect on a spell coherent at all, which the unqualified 405.4 does not give you. (b) The checkable consequence: as the final part of an instant or sorcery spell's resolution, the spell is put into its OWNER'S graveyard (608.2n), so commandeering an opponent's Demonic Tutor gets you the search but never the card. A permanent gained this way is likewise still owned by its owner (110.2, "A permanent's owner is the same as the owner of the card that represents it"; 108.3 for cards generally) — that corroboration is not required. A set that never distinguishes owner from controller has not answered "what does not change."

P6 — NOT REQUIRED, listed so a complete answer is recognizable. Controller is not a characteristic (109.3 lists "an object's owner or controller" among things that are not characteristics), so gaining control changes nothing about the spell's cost, colors, types or text, and costs already paid stay paid. Control change by itself also does not move the spell's targets — "The targets can't be changed except by another spell or ability that explicitly says it can do so" (115.1). Retargeting comes only from Commandeer's separate sentence, which says "choose new targets" and is therefore governed by 115.7d (you may leave any number unchanged even if illegal; any target you do change must be legal and must not make an unchanged target illegal), with 115.8 confirming you may not change a modal spell's mode and 115.7f that a divided effect's division is fixed.
