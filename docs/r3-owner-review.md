## What is being asked of the reviewer

Every question below is `contested` and every plan is `draft`, and both
stay that way until this review happens. Three separate decisions:

1. **Is the wording right?** The `clarified_ask` is what the plan was
   written against. If it does not say what the `ask` means, the plan is
   solving a different problem and its result is not evidence.
2. **Are the labelled cards right?** Required means every one is needed;
   any-of means one suffices; forbidden means returning it is wrong;
   counterexample means retrieving it is fine and recommending it is not.
3. **Is the plan expressing the ask, or fitting the answer?** A bound
   chosen after seeing where the answer ranked is tuning, not retrieval.

(1) PAYING 2 LIFE IS A PAYMENT OF THE MANA COST, NOT A REPLACEMENT FOR IT. {U/P} is one cost with two legal payment modes: one blue mana, or 2 life. A reader must be able to check that the life payment IS a payment of Mental Misstep's mana cost — not an alternative cost, not a "cast without paying its mana cost" effect, and not a way of leaving the cost unpaid. 107.4f states this directly (and incidentally fixes {U/P} as blue). (2) THE SPELL WAS STILL CAST. The payment-mode choice is an ordinary step of the casting procedure, so no step of casting is skipped or replaced, and all consequences of "cast" apply: it is a spell on the stack, cast-triggers trigger, it can be responded to and countered. Four rules each close this gap when paired with the required 107.4f, and ANY ONE suffices: 601.2b (names Phyrexian symbols and the 2-life announcement inside the casting steps — the strongest single citation), 118.13a (the choice for a symbol payable in multiple ways is made "as its controller proposes that spell", and the symbol is part of the spell's MANA COST), 601.2i (completing 601.2a–h makes the spell become cast and fires cast-triggers), or 601.2 itself (casting a spell INCLUDES determination and payment of costs — arguably the cleanest statement that paying life is part of casting rather than a substitute for it).

_Must establish._ A passage set answers this only if it establishes all four of the following checkable propositions.

(1) THE TEST. An activated ability is a mana ability only if it satisfies a specific conjunction of criteria, one of which is that it "could add mana to a player's mana pool when it resolves" (605.1a). Basalt Monolith's "{3}: Untap Basalt Monolith" is an activated ability whose entire effect is untapping a permanent; it can never put mana into a mana pool on resolution, so it fails that criterion. (The other 605.1a criteria — no target, not a loyalty ability, no card moved to or from a library — are all satisfied, so the mana-production criterion is the one that decides it.)

(2) THE TEST IS EXCLUSIVE, NOT MERELY SUFFICIENT. 605.1a is phrased as an "if", so on its own it cannot establish a negative. The set must also contain the exclusivity statement — either 605.1 ("Only abilities that meet either of the following two sets of criteria are mana abilities") or 605.5 ("Abilities that don't meet the criteria specified in rules 605.1a–b ... aren't mana abilities"). Either one alone is enough; this is what licenses the conclusion "the untap ability is NOT a mana ability." It also forecloses the two tempting rescues: that the permanent's OTHER ability is a mana ability, and that untapping indirectly leads to more mana later. Neither matters — the criteria are evaluated on the ability being activated, "regardless of what other effects they may generate."

(3) WHEN IT MAY BE ACTIVATED. Because it is an ordinary activated ability, the ordinary rule governs: it may be activated any time its controller has priority (117.1b), and it uses the stack like any other activated ability, so opponents may respond to it. A corollary a reader can derive: it cannot be activated during the untap step, since no player has priority then.

**Rules support** (`rules-support.2026-09-11.a`, set status proposed, label proposed unreviewed, confidence high)

_Must establish._ A passage set answers the clarified ask only if it establishes all of the following, each checkable:

(1) TARGET RESTRICTION — WHY A SPELL CAN BE TARGETED AT ALL. Spells are not targetable by default: only permanents are legal targets unless the effect explicitly targets an object that can't exist on the battlefield, such as a spell (115.2). Commandeer's "target noncreature spell" is exactly such an explicit case, so its target must be an object that is currently a spell — i.e. on the stack. Corroborating but not required: 109.2b ("a description of an object that includes the word 'spell' ... means a spell matching that description on the stack") pins that the target lives on the stack, and 608.2b/601.2c pin that the target is chosen at cast and rechecked on resolution, so Commandeer simply doesn't resolve if its target has already been countered or has resolved.

(2) TARGET RESTRICTION — WHAT "NONCREATURE" EXCLUDES. The restriction is a card-type restriction on the spell, not on what the spell does: any spell that is not a creature spell is legal (artifact, enchantment, planeswalker, battle, instant, sorcery spells), while a creature spell is illegal even when it also has another card type, such as an artifact creature spell. A set that only shows the damage-targeting rule (115.4) has not established this.

(3) WHAT CONTROL CHANGE DOES — THE EFFECT NOW HAPPENS FOR YOU. Because the spell's controller is now you, you follow its instructions as it resolves (608.2c) and/or every "you"/"your" in its text refers to you as its controller (109.5). Either citation carries this proposition; a set with neither leaves "you get the effect" unsupported.

(4) WHAT CONTROL CHANGE DOES — WHO GETS THE PERMANENT. If the commandeered spell is a permanent spell, you control the permanent it becomes (110.2b, corroborated by 608.3a: a resolving permanent spell "enters the battlefield under the control of the spell's controller"). The same rule also states the half that is easy to miss: the permanent's controller BY DEFAULT is still the player who put the spell on the stack, a distinction that matters in multiplayer (see 800.4c).

(5) WHAT CONTROL CHANGE DOES NOT CHANGE — OWNERSHIP. Control is not ownership. As the final part of an instant or sorcery spell's resolution, the spell is put into its OWNER'S graveyard (608.2n), so commandeering an opponent's Demonic Tutor does not give you the card; and a permanent you gain this way is still owned by its owner (108.3/112.2). A set that never distinguishes owner from controller has not answered "what does not change".

(6) WHAT CONTROL CHANGE DOES NOT CHANGE — THE SPELL ITSELF. Controller is not a characteristic (109.3), so gaining control changes nothing about the spell's cost, colors, types or text; costs already paid stay paid; and control change by itself does not move the spell's targets or its mode. Retargeting comes from Commandeer's separate sentence, governed by 115.7d ("choose new targets": you may leave any number unchanged even if illegal, and any target you do change must be legal), with 115.8 confirming that choosing new targets does not let you change a modal spell's mode and 115.7f that a divided effect's division is fixed. This clause is a useful addition to a complete answer but is not counted as required, since the clarified ask is scoped to the targeting restriction and the consequences of the control change.
