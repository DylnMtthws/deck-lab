Written by the synthesis agent of a ten-agent investigation, over three
diagnoses (rules-009, rules-010, and the 707.5 anomaly) and six grouping
transcriptions. Preserved verbatim.

**Acted on so far:** the chunker ceiling, the alternative-group transcription,
and three defects it found in `scripts/diagnose_rules_support.py`. **Not acted
on:** everything requiring a ruling — the plan-shape decomposition, the 202.3

the review packet.

Its numbers were measured against the PRE-fix index (400 chunks); the index is

**Finding: what "1 of 10" is actually measuring**

Nine of the ten rules questions fail. Only `rules-001` passes. Before anything is changed, the number needs to be attributed, because three separate defects are currently summing into one headline and two of them are not retrieval.

**1. Mostly the apparatus, not retrieval — with one honest unknown**

My estimate: the 1/10 is roughly one-third an index defect, one-third a plan-shape constraint, and one-third an answer-key encoding problem. Retrieval itself — the corpus, the embedding, the ranking — is close to exonerated, and the evidence for that is uniform across all three investigations: *every* required rule that has been re-queried in its own subject's vocabulary lands in the top 6 or better. 702.74a reaches rank 2. `CR 109.4b` reaches rank 3. 707.5's own text reaches rank 3. 115.2 reaches rank 1, 112.2 rank 1, 608.2n rank 3. Zero of the 61 scored quotes are unmatchable to a chunk. A retrieval system that returns the right passage whenever it is asked the right question is not a retrieval failure.

The decomposition of the nine failures, as best I can attribute it:

- **Index defect (decisive for 1, contributory for 3).** I re-measured this independently and it holds exactly: `/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/data/research-indexes/reference.db` has 400 chunks, **144 of them (36%) exceed the encoder's 510-token window**, and **45,314 of 212,715 corpus tokens (21%) are never read by the model**. The pin is at `/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/data/models/bge-small-en-v1.5/sentence_bert_config.json` (`max_seq_length: 512`). The worst chunk, labelled `CR 905.4a`, is 28,377 tokens and contains rule 905.3 onward *plus the entire Glossary plus the copyright credits* — which means the Glossary, the one part of the document written in the plain language these queries use, is effectively not in the index at all. Four of the fourteen missing required rules sit past their chunk's window (707.5 +205 tokens, 118.12 +217, 115.2 +28, 113.9 +15). The two highest-overlap misses in the whole table are exactly the two buried deepest. `rules-006` fails on this and essentially nothing else.
- **Plan shape (decisive for 2, contributory for most).** Eight of the fourteen missing rules rank at 20 or better — retrieval found them, and one 6-slot window had no room. `rules-009` is satisfiable *at the existing `limit: 6`* by three lookups instead of one. `rules-010` is not satisfiable by **any** single query at 6: concatenating the label's four required quotes verbatim — the maximally answer-fitted query that exists — still needs top_k=8. The four rules are topically orthogonal and one dense query is one point in embedding space. The IR already permits multiple `rules_lookup` steps; what forbids it is a comment in the header of `/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/fixtures/research/r3_plans/rules.yaml` saying "every plan here has the same two-step shape."
- **Answer-key encoding (decisive for at least 1, distorting for several).** `rules-007` fails with **every required rule covered** — it fails on its `sufficient_any_of` alone, and `/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/scripts/diagnose_rules_support.py` only enumerates missing *required* rules, so the diagnosis document explains eight of the nine failures and is silent about the ninth. Separately, `rules-005`'s 202.3 and `rules-008`'s 605.3a cannot independently fail (chunk-mates), four disjunctions are inert, the flat `sufficient_any_of` collapses two-group prose into one group in `_lift_flat_alternatives` (`/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/src/sabermetrics/assistant/eval/rules_support.py:175-179`), and the per-question bar ranges 2 to 4 distinct chunks. The key is simultaneously stricter than it should be in places and looser than its own prose in others.
- **Genuinely unexplained: one.** `rules-003`'s 104.3e. Overlap 0.00, never within 60, not truncation-affected. Its quote is *"An effect may state that a player loses the game."* — ten words. That is worth noticing on its own: required-rule quotes in this label set run from 10 to 61 words (median 32), so the overlap statistic's denominator varies sixfold and the 0.34/0.25/0.16 gradient is not comparing like with like. 104.3e is the one row where I would say we do not know.

One bookkeeping discrepancy to reconcile before quoting either number: the brief says 18 originally-missing required rules; the regenerated table in `/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/docs/r3-rules-support-diagnosis.md` lists 14 across 8 questions. Presumably pre- versus post-adjudication.

**What would settle it.** Nothing already in the repo, because every rank on record was obtained by asking where a known answer sits. Three things would: (a) fix the chunker and re-run, because that change is justified entirely by the model card and would be correct if all ten questions passed; (b) pre-register a plan decomposition rule, authored by a pass that has not seen the labels, applied uniformly to all ten; (c) write a *held-out* set of rules questions and labels against the same pinned document, sealed until after the fixes land. Only (c) actually settles it. These ten questions have now all been looked at; any number produced from them from here on is produced with knowledge of the answers, and should be reported that way.

**2. The structural tension: three things are being conflated**

The discipline that produced the failure is stated in the plans file header — "the rules question in plain language, with no card name in it at all" — and it is stricter than the rule the code enforces. `plans_naming_the_answer` (`/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/src/sabermetrics/assistant/eval/plans.py:236-285`) flags only card names drawn from the question's oracle ids that *the ask itself does not contain*. `rules-009`'s ask literally reads "When I evoke Endurance" — so "evoke" is the asker's word, and asking the corpus what evoke does is a lookup the asker requested. The plan strips it anyway and then cannot reach the only line in the entire document (normalized.txt:4564) that calls evoke's sacrifice a triggered ability. Three lines mention evoke at all; the glossary entry never says "triggered." The label is right and the question has no subject without it.

So separate these:

- **Naming the answer** — a rule number, a rule's distinctive wording, an oracle id, a card name the asker never said. Must stay forbidden. This is where 603.3b's *"multiple abilities have triggered since the last time a player received priority"* lives, and where a four-topic enumeration for `rules-010` lives.
- **Naming the subject the asker named** — "evoke", "Endurance", "Commandeer". Not a leak. The discipline should say so explicitly, because right now it does not and one plan is worse for it.
- **Naming a mechanic derivable from the ask or the card's printed text** — the grey zone. Permit it with a recorded derivation. Investigation 1 shows a paraphrase that never says "evoke" still reaches 702.74a at rank 16, so this is a legitimate paraphrase, not a crutch.

On the labels: `rules-009` is a discipline failure, not a label failure. `rules-010` is the opposite — 109.2b is a definitional link in a chain of authority with no stated stopping point, and its scoreable content (zone, and that "noncreature" filters printed types) is already carried by 112.1 and 112.3 inside chunk `CR 112`, which the label already requires and which the plan's own query already returns at rank 2. That demotion is defensible on redundancy grounds and only on redundancy grounds.

And underneath both there *is* a real limit: definitional rules are topically orthogonal to the questions that need them, and one dense vector cannot serve orthogonal topics. That limit is real, it is what makes `rules-010` unpassable at 6 with one query, and its remedy is architectural — more lookup steps — not a larger window.

**3. Order of work, and what must not be done**

**First, the chunker**, and only the chunker. `_split_by_size` (`/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/src/sabermetrics/reference_layer/chunker.py:512`) splits on `^(\d{3}\.\d+\w?)\s`, which requires whitespace after the number, so it fires on "707.2c " but never on "707.3." — producing one uninterruptible 5,116-character run. Make it accept the period form, make `target_chars` a hard ceiling below the encoder window, lift the Glossary into its own document, and label a chunk with the *first* rule it contains rather than the last (a chunk of 707.3–707.8 currently renders citations as `CR 707.2c`). Add a build-time refusal in `scripts/build_rules_index.py`: an index that silently discards 21% of its corpus should not be activatable. This escapes the answer-fitting trap completely — its acceptance criterion comes from a config file, not from a rank.

**Second, fix the instrument.** Two confirmed defects in `scripts/diagnose_rules_support.py`: the counterexample paragraph about 707.5 is hardcoded (~lines 222-226) and still prints "never ranks" at `--depth 100` where its own table reports rank 63; and it scores only `required_missing`, which is why `rules-007`'s failure is invisible. Add the column the overlap gradient is missing — *is this rule's quote inside its chunk's encoded window?* — and normalise or drop the overlap statistic given the sixfold denominator spread.

**Third, settle the label policy as policy**, re-issue as a new label-set id, keep the questions `contested`. The grouping transcription is ready to land and is a pure encoding correction: `sufficient_any_of` is already typed `list[list[str]]`, and for all six transcribed labels the union of the nested groups equals the existing flat list exactly. Note honestly that this makes the key *harder*, not easier — it un-inerts four disjunctions. Alongside it: one ruling on 202.3 across `rules-001`/`002`/`005`, a stated rule for where the chain of authority stops, and a decision on co-located requirements.

**Fourth and last, plan shape.** Derive the decomposition from the clarified ask's clause structure, pre-registered, applied to all ten. One caution: the clarified ask's propositions and the label's `sufficient_support` propositions are nearly the same list by design, which means decomposing by the label is copying the key while decomposing by the ask is not, and the two are hard to tell apart after the fact. The specific queries proposed in these investigations are *tainted* — they were authored knowing what they had to hit — and should be re-derived by a pass that has not seen the labels, even though the decomposition *rule* is clean.

**What must not be done.** Do not raise `limit` from 6. Every number that would work — 8, 15, 22, 28, 63 — was obtained by querying with the answer key's own words, and `deck-local-009` already demonstrated that a bound fitted to a key does not survive the key being corrected. Do not raise `PROBE_DEPTH` to shrink the "never ranks" bucket and call that progress; at depth 100 it moves from 4 to 2 and nothing has improved. Do not add the key's distinctive wording to any query — the card-name anti-cheat would not catch it, which is an argument for *extending* the check (refuse a plan whose `rules_lookup` text contains a ≥6-word span from that question's `quoted_evidence`) rather than for relying on discipline again. Do not drop 702.74a or 707.5 because they failed; both are verified unique carriers. Do not treat 707.5 as a retrieval bug and go tune the embedding model. And do not compare the post-rechunk score to 1/10: the content hash, embedding hash and generation id all change, so it is a different corpus and must be published as a new measurement.

**What we still do not know.** Whether a returned passage set actually *supports* an answer in prose — R3 has no narrator, and everything above measures only whether a quoted sentence appeared in a window. What a chunk's embedding would be if the model could read all of it (the 0.80 mean-pool figure is a proxy). Whether the counterfactual ranks survive a real re-chunk, since they swap one hypothetical chunk into the existing 400. Why 104.3e never ranks. And the provenance of the label decisions: no per-reviewer reports survive anywhere in the repo, so `/Users/dylan/Projects/deck_lab/worktrees/research-assistant/docs/r3-rules-support-critique.md` contradicts itself at lines 73 and 91 about which rule had two-of-three support, and neither version can be adjudicated. Fix that line to say "unverifiable"; do not lean on it for any decision.
